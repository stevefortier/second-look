# Example: qualities assigned to individual photos

This is the current input example, not a binary bird preference benchmark. Open `train/images/` to see the photos and `train/ratings.jsonl` to see their labels. Each row's `image` is relative to its split. `axes.yaml` defines the low, middle and high meaning of each arbitrary axis. To add a quality, define it there and rate or leave it `null` on each photo. No reference folder, A/B pairs, class label, caption or overall rating is required. `group` is optional and can keep related photographs together when splitting.

`liking` is just another optional quality. You can like a scary photo or dislike a beautiful one. Zero means a known midpoint; null means no judgment, not zero. Neither bird identity nor directory membership supplies an answer.

24 training and 8 held-out photos are checked in as original COCO JPEGs. These are the same group-disjoint images used for the small study 003 pilot, so `test/` is a **worked example**, not a new untouched benchmark for overnight model selection. Activity, crowding and color intensity are the study's visual proxy annotations. Liking, beauty, cuteness, scariness and humor are illustrative Scratch-authored judgments after viewing the photos: they are not Steve's ratings or objective ground truth. Replace them with your own to train your taste. `sources.jsonl` records source/creator links, per-image licenses and checksums; licenses differ. The legacy bird/control experiment is separately preserved in `datasets/legacy-coco/`.
